Wear the Rings

The Heesch problem: how many rings of copies of itself can a shape wear before a gap is forced? Surround the glowing tile with copies of it. No overlaps, every copy must touch what's already built, and a finished ring can't trap a hole. A complete ring locks, and the next one begins.

Your score is Yukon's: complete rings + the fraction of the next ring covered. The first three shapes can wear as many rings as you like. The last one is the current world record holder, a 15-cell polyhex that wears 4 rings and 251/254 of a fifth (4.9882). Press H to see the record corona ring by ring. On the night we made this game, our solver proved nothing with its inner rings can beat it.

Quantum bag (Q): the piece you get next is dealt by 32 certified random bytes measured on IBM's ibm_pittsburgh quantum computer (Bell S = 2.70, beyond the classical limit of 2). No rotating or flipping: place what the qubits give you.

The tiles are painted by Moth's Tessa engine: each colour becomes a point on a qubit's sphere and is read back through a quantum circuit. Controls: move to aim, click or tap to place, R rotate, F flip, Z undo.
